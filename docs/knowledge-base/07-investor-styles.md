# Learning from investors without pretending to clone them

Status: specification and research map; implementation not certified. Edition: 2026-10-05.

Study investors as decision frameworks and testable hypotheses. A public description of a successful investor omits judgment, execution history, access, constraints and failures. Quantropy should explain the process, expose assumptions and record what can actually be tested.

Value/business ownership: analyze durability, competitive position, capital allocation, normalized cash generation and purchase price. Quality/compounders: distinguish excellent economics from paying any price. Growth/GARP: analyze unit economics, growth runway and the expectations already embedded in price. Income: analyze payout sustainability, total return and tax rather than treating a large yield as safety. Passive indexing: diversify, control costs and implement a goal-consistent allocation. Macro/cycle investing: separate causal scenarios from retrospective stories. Distressed/special situations: model legal priority, catalysts, probabilities, timing and downside. Systematic styles: define measurable exposures and their costs rather than applying a famous name.

Primary starting points include Berkshire's [owner manual](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/1998ar/ownersmanual.html), Marks's [memos](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-best-of), and Vanguard's [principles](https://investor.vanguard.com/investor-resources-education/article/four-timeless-principles-for-investing-success). Lynch/Graham and other schools belong in a reading map; claims about them should cite original books or verified interviews before algorithmic codification.

13F-based copying has serious limits: reporting lags, excluded short positions and incomplete economic exposures. A disclosed holding is not a current recommendation or its holder's full risk position. Track reporting and availability dates, portfolio scope, amendments and disclosures. [SEC FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-13f).

Acceptance: style cards distinguish qualitative judgment, measurable screens and tested strategies; all performance comparisons use fair benchmarks and costs; no “Buffett algorithm” label implies replication of actual returns. Include a low-cost diversified baseline so active complexity has to justify itself.

## Coverage checklist

- Business ownership and value frameworks
- Quality growth and GARP frameworks
- Income sustainability analysis
- Passive allocation baseline
- Macro cycle and contrarian frameworks
- Distressed event-driven special situations
- Investor letters and reading maps
- 13F ownership tracking with lag
- Thesis falsification and decision journal
