# Banking, insurance institutions and financial stability

Banks transform deposits and other funding into loans and securities while supplying payment and credit services. Their assets can be illiquid and long-lived while liabilities are callable or short-lived. They therefore manage credit quality, interest-rate exposure, funding and capital together. Bank cash-flow interpretation differs from that of an industrial company.

Net interest income is interest revenue less interest expense; net interest margin divides an explicitly defined version by average earning assets. Loan losses reduce profits and capital; allowances are estimates rather than cash reserves in a separate vault. Capital absorbs losses, while liquidity resources meet cash demands. Regulatory capital and accounting equity differ.

Capital ratios relate eligible regulatory capital to risk-weighted assets; leverage measures use different exposure bases. The Basel framework separately addresses credit, market and operational risks and liquidity. Applicable implementation is jurisdiction- and date-dependent: an international framework page is not a current local rulebook. Do not hardcode one ratio as a universal legal minimum.

**Example.** A simplified bank has assets 100, liabilities 92 and equity 8. An unprovided asset loss of 3 reduces assets to 97 and equity to 5 if liabilities remain 92. A depositor withdrawal of 10 instead requires payment liquidity; asset sale discounts could then create additional capital losses. The balance-sheet identity does not ensure the bank can obtain cash quickly.

An insurer receives premiums and pays contingent claims, managing underwriting and investments jointly. Claims incurred can exceed claims paid because liabilities remain outstanding. A simplified property/casualty combined ratio adds loss and expense ratios under compatible premium bases; values below 100% indicate underwriting profit under that convention, before investment performance. Reserve adequacy, catastrophe aggregation, reinsurance recoverability and regulatory capital all matter.

Pension institutions manage obligations linked to wages, service, inflation and longevity. Funding ratios compare asset value to a liability valuation basis; changing discount conventions can change the ratio without a cash transaction. Asset-liability management considers duration, cash timing, inflation and downside funding needs rather than only asset returns.

Systemic risk comes from connections and shared exposures: runs, asset fire sales, contagion, collateral spirals and operational outages. Stress testing institutions individually can miss feedback between them. Resolution, deposit protection, central-bank facilities and supervision shape outcomes. This article supplies the conceptual bridge; regulatory ratios, actuarial reserves, ALM and systemic models still need detailed method contracts.
