# Decision quality, incentives, ethics and boundaries

Financial decisions combine objectives, beliefs, constraints and duties. A decision may be rational for one household or mandate and unsuitable for another with different liabilities, horizons or loss capacity. Document the purpose before selecting an optimization target. Risk tolerance is willingness to bear losses; risk capacity concerns the ability to sustain them; required return is a planning implication, not permission to assume unrealistic performance.

Behavioral patterns include overconfidence, anchoring, loss aversion, narrow framing, recency and confirmation bias. They are descriptive tendencies, not diagnoses. Rules such as precommitting decision criteria, keeping a research log, reviewing base rates and recording contrary evidence can improve process. They do not remove uncertainty.

Agency problems arise when a decision maker's incentives differ from those whose capital is at risk. Fees, commissions, personal holdings, benchmarking and career incentives can affect recommendations and research. Governance therefore includes conflicts, accountability, separation of approval and execution, and auditable records.

**Example.** A fund earns 8% before an illustrative 2% flat annual fee, leaving approximately 6% before other costs under a simple subtraction convention. Ten-year wealth on 100 would be about 215.89 at 8%, versus 179.08 at 6%. Actual fee timing and performance-fee waterfalls require exact contracts. Comparing gross model returns with net investor outcomes overstates usefulness.

Legal and ethical constraints include fair dealing, truthful communication, confidentiality, market integrity and conflicts. Tax avoidance, permitted tax planning and illegal evasion require jurisdiction-specific legal distinctions. Suitability and fiduciary duties vary with role and location; a general educational page cannot supply a universal binding standard.

Research should report negative results, distinguish observations from assumptions, and avoid marketing hypotheses as established returns. A named investor's letters can inform a framework without enabling a faithful clone of their tacit decisions. Forecast uncertainty should not be hidden behind excessive decimal places or a smooth simulation chart.

The knowledge base must distinguish explanation, estimate, implementation capability and investment recommendation. A method can be mathematically valid but economically inappropriate, operationally inaccessible or legally restricted. Advanced estate, philanthropy, cross-border planning, sustainable finance, governance and conduct requirements remain dated specialist units.
