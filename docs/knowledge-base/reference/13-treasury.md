# Treasury, payments and commercial finance

Treasury manages an organization's cash, funding, financial exposures and banking relationships. A profitable business can run out of cash when collections lag costs, debt matures, or collateral calls arrive. A cash forecast tracks bank balances, expected collections, payroll, taxes, capital investment, debt service and committed facilities by date and currency.

Working-capital finance includes trade credit, receivables factoring, inventory finance and supplier-finance arrangements. Distinguish genuine transfer of risk from borrowing secured by assets. Commercial contracts introduce performance, documentary, counterparty and country risk; a guarantee or letter of credit is not identical to immediate cash or an unconditional sovereign claim.

**Example.** Opening cash 20, customer collections 30, operating payments 35 and debt service 10 imply closing cash 5. A collection delay of 12 turns that into -7 before additional funding. An annual positive profit forecast would not reveal the specific payment shortfall. Cash pooling, facility draw limits and currency restrictions need explicit treatment.

A payment lifecycle can include initiation, authentication, authorization, clearing and settlement. Clearing determines obligations; settlement discharges them through asset transfer under the system's rules. A notification or matched instruction alone may not mean legally final payment. Delivery-versus-payment and payment-versus-payment arrangements address principal risk by linking transfers.

Market infrastructure includes payment systems, securities depositories, securities settlement systems, central counterparties and repositories. Credit, liquidity, custody, operational and legal risks require distinct controls. A central counterparty reallocates counterparty risk rather than eliminating all risk. Collateral valuation, margin and default-management arrangements can affect participants' funding needs.

Treasury hedging starts with an exposure: a future foreign-currency payment, floating-rate debt, commodity purchase or contingent sale. Hedge size and maturity should follow that exposure and objectives. A forward that reduces price uncertainty may still create collateral cash demands; option premiums trade upfront cost for asymmetric protection. Economic hedging and qualification for hedge accounting are different tests.

Detailed cash forecasting, trade finance, debt covenant models, project finance waterfalls, merchant acquiring, payment fraud and treasury control design remain specialist units. The reference uses illustrative mechanics, not legal interpretations of actual contracts.
