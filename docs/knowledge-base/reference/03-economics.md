# Economics, monetary systems and public finance

Economics studies allocation under scarcity. Microeconomic analysis covers demand, supply, elasticities, market structure and externalities. Macroeconomic analysis covers aggregate output, employment, prices, money, credit and cross-border accounts. These descriptions explain channels; they do not supply a reliable mechanical trading signal.

Nominal GDP measures output at current prices; real GDP removes an estimated price effect. Inflation is a rate of change in a defined price index, not the price level. A household's experienced inflation can differ from a national basket. Policy rates, borrowing rates and long bond yields differ because of term structure, credit risk and other market conditions. Realized real rates and expected real rates also differ.

A simplified sovereign debt identity is B_t=(1+i)B_(t-1)-PB_t+SFA_t. B is nominal debt, i the effective interest rate on existing debt, PB the primary surplus (revenue less non-interest expenditure), and SFA stock-flow adjustments such as valuation or recognition changes. Dividing by current GDP, with nominal GDP growth g, gives b_t=[(1+i)/(1+g)]b_(t-1)-pb_t+sfa_t. Use consistent debt and government boundaries.

**Example.** Initial debt/GDP 60%, effective nominal interest 4%, nominal GDP growth 3%, primary surplus 1% of current GDP, and zero stock-flow adjustment give next debt/GDP 59.582524%. This is an accounting scenario, not a sustainability certification. Currency mismatch, refinancing, maturity, contingent liabilities and policy responses can dominate it.

Credit creation and payments connect household and corporate balance sheets with banks, the central bank and government. Solvency concerns net worth and future obligations; liquidity concerns ability to pay when due. A solvent borrower can fail from lack of funding. Financial-system analysis therefore includes institutions, supervision, crisis management and infrastructure rather than market prices alone.

FX quotes need an orientation. If X means domestic currency per foreign unit, an increase in X is domestic depreciation. Unhedged domestic gross return on a foreign asset is (1+r_foreign)(X_1/X_0). Interest parity relations depend on funding, collateral and transaction assumptions; deviations can reflect balance-sheet constraints rather than a free executable arbitrage.

**Unfinished specialist coverage.** General equilibrium, growth theory, international monetary systems, monetary transmission, sovereign restructuring, municipal finance, development finance, tax incidence, public project appraisal and distributional welfare each need separate reference units. Government financing capacity is not identical to a household budget. Political and institutional constraints belong in a scenario, not an unqualified equation.
