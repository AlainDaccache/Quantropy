# Economics, macro scenarios and market structure

Status: specification and research map; implementation not certified. Edition: 2026-10-05.

Economic knowledge should support interpretation and explicit scenarios even when reliable prediction is unavailable. Provide a causal map of how demand, supply, production costs, competition, policy, growth and inflation may affect issuer cash flows, discount rates and markets. Record mechanisms and alternative explanations instead of converting a macro narrative into an automatic buy/sell signal.

At the business level, model unit economics, price/volume, demand sensitivity, fixed/variable costs and competitive responses. At the macro level, maintain dashboards for output, labor, inflation, policy, credit conditions, trade and external balances with source vintages. Compare nominal and real measures and distinguish levels from growth rates. Forecasting should use only historically available releases, with publication lag and revisions visible.

FX analysis needs cross-rates, spread-aware conversion, forward valuation and parity relationships with consistent quote orientation. Covered relationships depend on executable financing/transaction conditions; an apparent spreadsheet arbitrage may disappear after bid/ask, constraints and funding. Uncovered parity and purchasing-power ideas are hypotheses about economic behavior, not enforceable near-term price identities.

Add policy-shock, productivity, demographics, commodity supply, geopolitical and climate-transition scenarios. Associate sensitivities and uncertainty with assets/businesses, avoiding a falsely exact macro-to-price mapping. Stress alternative narratives and identification limitations. Market structure should explain auction/continuous trading, fragmented venues, dealer markets, liquidity and why retail orders interact differently with each.

Acceptance: release vintages reproduce past dashboards; time-series transformations carry units; FX triangles include quote spreads; scenario assumptions and exposures are traceable. Qualitative economics becomes structured scenario evidence with citations and confidence, not a made-up numerical truth. CFA economics provides a coverage baseline; the detailed macro-model specification needs its own original statistical and economic references. [Level I outline](https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/docs/programs/cfa-program/2026-l1-topics-combined.pdf), [Level II outline](https://www.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/docs/programs/cfa-program/2026-l2-topics-combined.pdf).

## Coverage checklist

- Business unit economics and competition
- Demand price volume and cost scenarios
- Growth inflation and policy dashboards
- Release-vintage macro forecasting
- FX parity cross-rates and forward valuation
- Trade external balance and country risk
- Geopolitical supply and transition scenarios
- Market structure auctions dealers and fragmentation
- Qualitative causal maps and uncertainty
