# Corporate finance and valuation

Value is the discounted economic benefit attached to a claim under specified assumptions. Price is a transaction or quote. Corporate finance asks which investments to undertake, how to finance them, and how to distribute capital. Use incremental after-tax project cash flows: include opportunity costs and working capital, exclude sunk costs, and prevent financing costs from being counted both in cash flow and the discount rate.

For a conventional non-financial operating business, define FCFF=EBIT(1-t)+D&A-capex-change in operating net working capital. This assumes the tax simplification is appropriate and capex is a gross cash investment measure. Model acquisitions separately or include them explicitly in reinvestment; do not assume an acquisition-led growth forecast costs nothing. If reinvestment is already defined net of D&A, do not add D&A a second time.

Enterprise operating value=sum(FCFF_t/(1+WACC)^t)+discounted terminal value. WACC=w_e k_e+w_d k_d(1-t) in the simple debt/equity case uses market-value weights and applicable tax-shield assumptions. Equity value bridges from operating value by adding non-operating assets and subtracting senior claims. FCFE discounted at cost of equity directly values common equity under a consistent leverage policy. APV instead separates unlevered project value from financing effects. Do not combine these approaches inconsistently.

**Example.** EBIT 100, tax rate 25%, D&A 10, gross capex 20 and working-capital investment 5 give FCFF=60. Constant next-year FCFF 60, WACC 8% and perpetual growth 2% imply a stylized operating value of 1,000. That terminal assumption requires sustainable reinvestment, competitive economics and g<WACC; it is not justified by the algebra alone.

Residual income values equity as current book value plus discounted future earnings above an equity capital charge, under consistent clean-surplus adjustments. Relative valuation compares claims using matched economic denominators and comparable risk/growth/accounting; comparability is an argument to establish, not an industry label. Asset-based valuation estimates asset values less obligations, often useful in liquidation or asset-heavy settings.

Banks need an equity and regulatory-capital perspective because deposits and funding are part of operations; ordinary industrial working capital is poorly suited. Insurers require underwriting, reserve adequacy, invested assets and capital constraints. Conglomerates require a sum of parts with shared costs, tax and cross-holdings reconciled once. Serial acquirers require acquisition spending, integration economics, goodwill and dilution. REIT/property analysis distinguishes property cash flow, maintenance investment, leverage and entity claims. Resource firms require depletion, development and commodity scenarios. Pre-revenue firms need runway, dilution and probability-weighted milestones.

These are routing principles, not finished sector models. Forecast drivers, comparable normalization, distress/default, dilution and scenario sensitivity must be specified for each application. A discounted-cash-flow model is a structured forecast, not a precise observation of value.
