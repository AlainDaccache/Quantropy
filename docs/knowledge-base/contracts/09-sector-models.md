# Sector economics and valuation input profiles

Status: substantive draft; author review only. Examples are fictional. This is a family contract: named advanced variants are not fully specified unless their procedure appears explicitly below. Implementation is unassessed.

Prerequisites: [08-valuation](08-valuation.md).

## Bank and insurer profiles

For a bank, forecast loan/deposit balances, earning yields, funding costs, fee income, operating expense, credit losses and required capital. Distributable equity cash flow can be normalized common income less incremental required equity capital, with releases constrained by capital and management policy. Residual-income or dividend models provide an equity-centered cross-check. Example assets/risk exposures require capital rising from 10 to 12 and income is 3: distribution capacity is 1 before other constraints. Do not subtract deposits as conventional industrial net debt.

P&C insurance separates premiums earned, claims incurred, expense, reserve development and investment results. Combined ratio=loss ratio+expense ratio only after reconciling denominators; written-premium expense ratios and earned-premium loss ratios are common but not algebraically identical to one total expense/revenue measure. Float is financing associated with obligations, not unrestricted surplus cash. Life insurance needs mortality, lapse, guarantees, credited rates and asset/liability valuation. Reserve releases cannot be assumed recurring distributable profit.

## Property and digital-business profiles

Property forecasts occupancy, rents, operating costs, maintenance/renewal capex, lease terms and financing. FFO is a defined supplemental REIT performance measure with real-estate depreciation and gain/impairment adjustments; consult the applicable Nareit definition. AFFO is not one universally standardized cash-flow measure: reconcile issuer maintenance capex, leasing and other adjustments. NOI/cap-rate values property-level income and still needs the entity equity bridge.

Subscription businesses use cohort revenue, churn, expansion, gross margin and acquisition spend. NRR=(opening cohort revenue-churn-contraction+expansion)/opening cohort revenue; GRR excludes expansion. Example opening cohort 100, churn 10, contraction 5 and expansion 20 give NRR 105% and GRR 85%. CAC payback=acquisition spend/new recurring gross profit per period under matched cohort definitions. Simplified LTV gross profit/churn assumes stable rates and infinite persistence; discounting and cohort survival improve the model.

## Other profiles

Marketplaces model transaction value*take rate and distinguish pass-through volume from recognized revenue. Resource firms use production, price, unit cost, depletion, development and closure liabilities. Utilities require permitted revenue/return, rate base, capex and regulatory timing. Early-stage biotech uses conditional milestone probabilities, cash runway, dilution and commercialization scenarios; multiplying marginal probabilities without dependency assumptions is wrong. Asset managers use AUM, net flows, market returns, fee mix and operating leverage. Brokers additionally require client assets, funding, capital and counterparty constraints.

Conglomerates apply these profiles by segment and reconcile parent costs and financing. Distressed companies add default, restructuring priority and recovery. These driver profiles are actionable draft inputs, not issuer-specific calibrated models; accounting/regulatory mappings and market examples require review.
