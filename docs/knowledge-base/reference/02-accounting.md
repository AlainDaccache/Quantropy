# Accounting, statements and analytical metrics

Accounting describes an entity through a reporting boundary and recognition, measurement and disclosure policies. Assets equal liabilities plus equity. Profit is an accrual measure; cash generation is a movement in cash. Revenue can precede collection, expenses can precede payment, and capitalization can defer expense recognition. Read the notes before interpreting a ratio.

The balance sheet is a stock at a date. The income statement and cash-flow statement cover an interval. Equity changes through profit, distributions, owner contributions and other recognized movements. Cash opening balance plus operating, investing and financing flows, plus relevant currency effects, reconciles to closing cash under the entity's reporting presentation.

**Canonical basic metrics.** Gross margin=(revenue-COGS)/revenue; operating margin=EBIT/revenue; net margin=net income/revenue. Current ratio=current assets/current liabilities. ROE=income attributable to common shareholders/average common equity. ROA needs an explicit income numerator and average asset denominator. ROIC=NOPAT/average invested capital, with operating/non-operating classifications stated. NOPAT here means EBIT times (1-normalized operating tax rate), a simplifying analytical convention, not an accounting line item.

**Example.** Revenue 200, COGS 120, EBIT 30 and net income 18 give margins 40%, 15% and 9%. Average common equity 90 gives ROE 20%. If beginning receivables are 20 and closing receivables are 35, that increase consumes 15 cash relative to otherwise identical recognized sales. It does not itself prove poor collection; examine sales growth, aging and allowances.

Working-capital analysis separates operating receivables, inventories and payables from cash and financing balances. Days-sales-outstanding commonly uses average receivables/revenue times days; inventory days use average inventory/COGS; payable days preferably use purchases when available. Cash-conversion cycle=receivable days+inventory days-payable days. A negative cycle can be structurally healthy; classification and seasonality matter.

P/E=price per share/compatible EPS; EV multiples pair enterprise value with a pre-financing operating denominator. EV adjustments can include debt, preferred interests, non-controlling interests and non-operating cash, but require consistent treatment of leases, pensions and investments. Negative or tiny denominators make many multiples misleading. A low multiple alone is not undervaluation.

Separate restated economic analysis from the version actually available on a historical research date. Store original and normalized facts, units, periods, filing timestamps and transformations. Never mix quarterly flows, annual flows and instantaneous balances by accident. IFRS and US GAAP differences, sector-specific accounting, and forensic score coefficients remain detailed research tasks. Altman, Beneish and Piotroski scores are screens with defined populations and conventions, not verdicts about fraud or solvency.
