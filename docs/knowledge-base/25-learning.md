# Learning path and vocabulary

Status: specification and research map; implementation not certified. Edition: 2026-10-05.

Learn in a sequence that connects concepts to decisions. First: compounding, real/nominal return, inflation, opportunity cost, diversification, liquidity, leverage and market orders. Next: accounting statements, cash versus earnings, working capital and valuation assumptions. Then: instrument cash flows, duration, futures settlement, option payoffs and hedging. Follow with return/benchmark accounting, covariance, empirical inference and time-aware research. Only then add complex optimization, volatility surfaces, advanced ML and automated execution.

Important distinctions: price is a market observation; value is a conditional estimate. An asset class is an economic category; an instrument is a legal contract. A signal is an estimate or rule input; a strategy includes position and trading decisions. Exposure is economic sensitivity; margin is collateral required by a venue/broker. Volatility measures dispersion; risk includes goal failure and operational loss. Backtest is a historical simulation; validation is a broader evidence process. Paper trading tests integration with simulated fills; live trading creates actual obligations.

Point-in-time means information availability at the decision moment. Survivorship bias means selectively retaining assets that survived. Look-ahead leakage means using unavailable information. Overfitting means selecting patterns too specific to the searched sample. A risk-neutral measure is a pricing tool; it is not automatically a forecast of real-world probabilities. Implied volatility comes from market option prices under a model; realized volatility is calculated from observed returns under a measurement convention.

Build interactive lessons around the worked fixtures: change a DCF growth assumption, alter a bond curve, shock a volatility surface, add a portfolio deposit, increase futures leverage and interrupt an order. Show assumptions and consequences, not just equations. Offer beginner explanations alongside full technical definitions.

Source-backed reading starts with SEC statements, exchange/industry instrument education, Damodaran valuation materials, original empirical papers and broker references. Book lists and advanced derivations should be added after edition/full-text review. This base is a curriculum map, not a substitute for all those texts.

## Coverage checklist

- Foundations and compounding lessons
- Statement and valuation lessons
- Instrument payoff and settlement lessons
- Portfolio accounting and risk lessons
- Research inference and causality lessons
- Advanced pricing and execution lessons
- Plain-language glossary and worked exercises
