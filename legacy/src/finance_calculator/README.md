## Pain Points
What are current solutions not addressing?
What do they impact (accuracy, user experience...)?

1. Volatile Economic Conditions: Traditional retirement calculators often overlook the variability in economic conditions, assuming stable withdrawal rates. This can lead to inaccurate projections as equity and bond returns, as well as inflation, can vary significantly. Retirees may face the risk of running out of money sooner than expected. Furthermore, these calculators fail to account for the impact of early retirement spending preferences and the occurrence of a recession in the initial years of retirement.

2. Changing Retirement Landscape: The traditional rules of thumb for retirement, such as a 4% safe withdrawal rate and retiring at age 65, may no longer be applicable in today's evolving retirement landscape. Many individuals, particularly younger generations, are rethinking retirement and seeking early financial independence. This paradigm shift necessitates a retirement calculator that can adapt to different retirement timelines and accommodate unconventional retirement goals.

3. Rising Life Expectancy: Increasing life expectancies mean that retirees need to plan for longer retirement periods. Traditional calculators often underestimate the required savings and withdrawal rates needed to sustain a longer retirement. It is crucial to consider longevity risk and ensure that retirement plans can support a comfortable lifestyle throughout an extended retirement.

4. Inflation and Healthcare Costs: Inflation and healthcare costs are significant factors that can erode the purchasing power of retirees over time. Retirement calculators should incorporate realistic assumptions about inflation rates and rising healthcare expenses to accurately estimate future income needs and the required savings. Failing to account for these factors can result in underestimated retirement goals and potential financial difficulties later in life.

5. Sequence of Returns Risk: The sequence of investment returns during the early years of retirement can have a substantial impact on the sustainability of a retiree's portfolio. Traditional calculators often assume a static average return, disregarding the potential for negative returns early in retirement. Incorporating sequence of returns risk analysis can provide a more realistic assessment of portfolio performance and help retirees make informed decisions about withdrawal rates and asset allocation adjustments during retirement.

6. Changing Social Security Landscape: The Social Security system is subject to potential changes in regulations and funding. Retirement calculators should consider the impact of potential alterations to Social Security benefits and help retirees plan accordingly to mitigate any potential shortfalls. This includes exploring strategies for optimizing Social Security claiming decisions and adapting retirement plans based on evolving social security policies.

By addressing these additional pain points, an advanced retirement calculator can offer a more comprehensive and accurate assessment of retirement readiness, enabling individuals to make informed decisions, adjust their plans as needed, and achieve their retirement goals with greater confidence.

The safe withdrawal rate (SWR) method is one way that retirees can determine how much money they can withdraw from their accounts each year without running out of money before reaching the end of their lives.

The safe withdrawal rate method is a conservative approach that tries to balance having enough money to live comfortably with not depleting retirement savings prematurely. It is based largely on the portfolio’s value at the beginning of retirement.
The safe withdrawal rate (SWR) method calculates how much a retiree can draw annually from their accumulated assets without running out of money prior to death.
The SWR method employs conservative assumptions, including spending needs, the rate of inflation, and how much annual return investments will return.
One problem with SWR is that it projects economic and financial conditions at retirement to continue as-is into the future, when in fact they can change in the years or decades after retirement.
Figuring out how to use your retirement savings isn’t easy because there are so many unknowns, including how the market will perform, how high inflation will be, whether you will develop additional expenses (such as medical), and your life expectancy. The longer you expect to live, the longer the time period you need to cover, which means you may experience more "unknowns" or factors that you can't control. In addition, the worse the market performs, the more likely you are to run out of money.
